Metric quality journeys

Twelve journeys through metric quality as revised on 15 November, walked step by step with the demo workspace's people and metrics. Each one says who does it and how often, what Faro shows at each step, what it costs them in time, and what the walk found. The point is to catch the workflow's gaps and burdens before anything is built.

Companion to the metric quality sketches. Times are estimates for a confident user, not measurements.

The lifecycle in one picture

What the journeys found

The revised design holds up: a goal owner's weekly work doesn't change, and the extra work falls on metric owners and verifiers, a few times a year. The walk found eight gaps. Lane decided four on 15 November, each as recommended; the other four were settled as details.

  1. Lane decidedA verification that fails has nowhere to go. When the reproduced figure doesn't match, the design has only "Mark as Verified". The verifier should be able to record "Couldn't reproduce" with their figure and a note, so the owner sees it and the next verifier doesn't start blind (journey 4).
  2. Lane decidedBaselines outlive the source they were taken from. After a source change, the stored baselines came from the old system and may not match readings from the new one. The goal would then show a jump that is the source, not the work (journey 5).
  3. Lane decidedA metric that refreshes monthly makes a weekly goal look neglected. Cost per 1,000 requests refreshes monthly, so three weeks in four the owner has nothing new to report. Refresh is now a required field, so the goal's check-in rhythm could follow it, which is MC's job, pulled forward (journey 8).
  4. Lane decidedA metric's owner can leave. Owner is required for Defined, so a metric whose owner leaves would drop to Draft and warn on every goal it measures, though nothing about the number changed (journey 11).
  5. SettledVerifying with no baseline. Baselines are no longer needed for Defined, so a verifier may have nothing stored to compare with. They pick a period; Faro shows the stored baseline for it if there is one, otherwise the latest reading from the source (NR), otherwise only records the figure (journey 3).
  6. SettledDuplicates. Adding a metric checks names, source and meaning against the library and offers "Use this one". Two that already exist are merged by retiring one with "replaced by" (MT3), so goals move across (journey 10).
  7. SettledRe-verification arrives in waves. A library set up in one month lapses in one month a year later. "To verify" shows a metric 30 days before it lapses ("due 3 Nov"), so the verifiers spread the work over the month (journey 12).
  8. SettledQuarterly baselines are the biggest routine chore. Where a source can be read, "Take this quarter's baselines" fills every slice's value and period from the readings for the owner to confirm in one go. Where it can't, the owner types them in (journey 6).

Every journey through MCP

Each journey can be done from an AI tool without opening Faro. The tools call the same rules as the screens, ask through checks wherever a screen would ask, and refuse in words where a screen would refuse. Two tools are new (save_baselines, verify_metric); the rest extend today's.

1

Set a goal on a number nobody has defined yet

Rhea Kapoor, CI/CD manager · week 1 of a cycle · once per new metric

  1. Rhea creates "Flaky test rate in the main pipelines" and opens Measured by. She types "flaky"; nothing matches, so the picker offers Add a metric "flaky".
  2. A short form asks only for the name, unit and direction, the minimum to save. The metric is saved as Draft and measures her goal at once.
  3. The goal page shows the warning "Measured by a Draft metric" and a Finish defining it link. Her goal's status works as normal.
  4. Later that week she opens the metric, picks Ratio, writes the numerator and denominator, sets "rolling 7 days" and "refreshed daily", and names CI test analytics. The "still needed" box counts down to one item: the report link.
  5. Aiko sends her the dashboard link; she pastes it and the metric becomes Defined by itself. The warning disappears from her goal.
Cost

About 1 minute on the day, 10–15 minutes later in the week. Goal setting never waits.

Found

Adding a metric from inside the picker must ask for no more than name, unit and direction, or people will avoid it and leave the goal unmeasured.

2

Load a 60-row metrics register

Lucía Romero, engineering analyst · once, at set-up · by spreadsheet or through an AI tool (MCP)

  1. Lucía fills in the workbook's Metrics sheet: name, meaning, unit, direction, and whichever of the structured columns the register has (what is measured, aggregation, numerator, denominator, window, source link, refresh).
  2. The import's check says, before anything is saved: "60 metrics: 9 will be Defined, 51 Draft. 3 look like metrics you already have." It lists the three with "Use the existing one" chosen.
  3. Rows that can't be parsed into the structure (for example "window: last quarter-ish") keep their words in the meaning and land as Draft, with the missing part named.
  4. After the load, Metrics › Draft 51 is her work list, sorted by how many goals each one measures, so the ones that matter most come first.
  5. The same load through an AI tool (bulk_change with metrics) returns the same summary in words, and each metric's missing list, so the tool can ask for the missing links in one go.
Cost

1–2 hours for the sheet, then the Drafts over a few weeks, shared with the owners.

Found

Nothing is lost when a row is incomplete; it lands as Draft. The duplicate check runs on import too, not only on the add form.

3

Verify a metric

Amara Nwosu, VP and admin · whenever "To verify" has something · 10–20 minutes each

  1. Home shows Amara "2 metrics to verify". She opens CI pipeline time on main (p90), owned by Daniel. She is neither its owner nor its last editor, so Verify is offered.
  2. She opens the report link in a new tab. It shows the p90, rolling 7 days, for main.
  3. Faro asks for a period. The metric has a company baseline for Aug–Oct 2026 (22 min), so it suggests that period. She reads 21 min from the dashboard and enters it.
  4. Faro shows "1 min lower than stored" in the metric's own unit, with no pass mark. She adds a note: "Matches within rounding; Sept's runner outage week is in both."
  5. She marks it Verified. Daniel is told; the record shows her name, the date, both figures, the note and "due again 15 Nov 2027".
Cost

10 minutes for a metric with a clean dashboard.

Found

With no stored baseline, Faro falls back to the latest reading from the source, then to no comparison at all. The figure is still required; it is what makes the record mean something.

4

A verification that doesn't match

Lucía Romero, named verifier · Cloud cost per 1,000 requests, owned by Björn Halvorsen

  1. Lucía opens the FinOps dashboard for October and gets $0.41. Faro has $0.38 stored for October.
  2. She reads the caveat: committed-use discounts land in the month they're bought. October was a purchase month, and the dashboard spreads the discount over the year; the stored figure didn't.
  3. The figures don't match and she can't tell which is right, so she chooses Couldn't reproduce with her figure and a note: "Dashboard amortises the CUD; the stored baseline doesn't. Which does the definition mean?"
  4. The metric stays Defined. Björn is told. The metric's page shows the attempt: who, when, both figures, the note.
  5. Björn adds "discounts amortised over 12 months" to the exclusions and retakes the baseline. The next verifier sees Lucía's attempt and checks that point first.
Cost

20 minutes for Lucía, 15 for Björn. It found a real ambiguity in the definition, which is the feature working.

Lane decidedRecord "Couldn't reproduce" with the figure and note; the metric stays Defined and the owner is told.
5

The source changes

Pavel Novák moves Lead time for changes from Four Keys to deploy pipeline events · Rhea owns the metric

  1. Pavel edits the source and link and gives a reason. Before saving, Faro says: "This changes the source, so the metric will no longer be Verified, and 1 goal is measured by it."
  2. He saves. The metric drops to Defined, joins "To verify", and Rhea (owner) is told. Pavel can't verify it: he made the change.
  3. The company baseline (31 hours, Jul–Sep) came from Four Keys. Deploy events will read higher, because they count to production, not to merge.
  4. The goal's chart shows a mark at week 46, "Source changed: deploy pipeline events", so a step in the line is read as the source and not as a bad week.
  5. The stored baselines are shown as "taken from the old source". Rhea is asked to retake them from the new one, and the goal is asked whether to start from the retaken figure.
Cost

2 minutes for Pavel, 10 for Rhea to retake baselines, a re-verification by someone else.

Lane decidedFlag the old baselines as "taken from the old source" and ask the owner to retake them; the chart marks the change too.
6

A new quarter, new baselines

Marisol Duarte, Director of Reliability · first week of each quarter · her four metrics

  1. Home shows "4 of your metrics have no baseline for Oct–Dec". She opens Metrics, filtered to hers.
  2. Take this quarter's baselines lists each metric and slice with a value already filled from the source's readings, the period, today's date and "read from Four Keys" as how it was taken.
  3. She corrects one figure where the incident tracker was late, gives a reason, and confirms the rest in one go.
  4. The old baselines move to History, each with "replaced by Oct–Dec 2026". Every metric stays Verified: a baseline is a reading.
  5. Goals already running keep their start values. Goals she sets this quarter start from the new figures.
Cost

About 5 minutes for four metrics when the sources can be read; 15 if she types the figures in.

Found

This is the most frequent chore in the whole feature. Without the readings pre-fill it is the one most likely to be skipped, leaving stale baselines.

7

A team wants its own slice

Pavel Novák, Build & Release · a team goal on the company's Change failure rate

  1. Pavel sets "Change failures in our services" and picks Change failure rate, measured for Build & Release, part "tier-1 services".
  2. There is no baseline for that slice. The goal asks for its start value as usual and offers Also save it as the slice's baseline, with the period and how it was taken.
  3. He ticks it. The metric's page now lists a Build & Release (tier-1 services) baseline beside the company's. The metric stays Verified.
  4. His goal and Marisol's company goal share one definition, so the review can put 9.4% beside 11.8% and both mean the same thing.
Cost

About 1 extra minute, folded into setting the goal.

Found

A slice is a team plus an optional part (MT2), not only a team. Taking the baseline from inside the goal avoids a second visit to the metric.

8

The weekly check-in

Mateo Fernández (Mean time to restore, refreshed weekly) and Ethan Brooks (Cloud cost per 1,000 requests, refreshed monthly)

  1. Mateo's check-in opens with this week's reading filled in: "From the incident tracker, 58 min, read Monday". He confirms it and writes his story. Metric quality adds nothing to his week.
  2. Ethan's opens in week 2 of the month with "No new reading: Cloud cost refreshes monthly, next on 1 Dec".
  3. Today he must still post something or the goal turns "No recent update" after two weeks, though there is nothing new to say.
  4. With refresh now required, Faro knows this. The goal could take its rhythm from the metric: due monthly, after each refresh, and never "No recent update" in between.
Cost

Nothing for Mateo. Ethan today: three empty check-ins a month.

Lane decidedA goal's check-in rhythm follows its metric's refresh, as part of MQ2 (MC's core, pulled forward).
9

Trusting the number in a review

Amara Nwosu, the Reliability weekly · agenda of six goals

  1. An agenda row shows the goal's status and number as today, with one quiet line underneath: "Change failure rate · Verified 3 Nov · refreshed weekly".
  2. Mean time to detect's row says "Draft metric" with the warning mark. Amara asks Yuki what's missing, and the answer is on the metric page: the window.
  3. Daniel claims the deploy count is the real cause of the change-failure rise. Both numbers are Verified, so the argument is about the work, not the numbers.
  4. Nothing in the review lets anyone change a metric. The meeting asks; owners change things afterwards.
Cost

None. This is where the value shows up: less time arguing about whose number is right.

Found

Verified must be visible where decisions are made (review rows, goal pages), or it is library housekeeping nobody sees.

10

Two people define the same number

Tariq Haddad adds "Deploy frequency" · the library already has Production deploys per day

  1. As Tariq types the name and source, Faro shows "Similar: Production deploys per day (Verified, DORA dashboard, Pavel Novák)" with Use this one.
  2. He reads its meaning: successful production deploys per working day, 28 days. It is what he means, so he picks it. No second metric is made.
  3. Had both existed already (two teams' copies from a register), an admin retires one with "replaced by" the other (MT3). Its goals' owners are offered the replacement; their history keeps a mark where the definition changed.
Cost

Saves a duplicate that would otherwise need defining, verifying and baselining twice.

Found

The duplicate check is worth more than any field on the form: one definition per number is the whole point.

11

A metric's owner leaves

Rhea Kapoor moves to another division · she owns four metrics

  1. An admin removes Rhea from the workspace. Her four metrics, including Lead time for changes, now have no owner.
  2. Owner is one of the ten parts of Defined, so as designed all four would drop to Draft, and five goals would show the warning.
  3. Nothing about the numbers has changed, so that warning would be wrong.
  4. Better: ownership passes to the person's team lead (CI/CD's new manager), each metric is listed under "To sort out: new owner, please confirm", and the stage doesn't move.
Cost

2 minutes per metric for the new owner to confirm.

Lane decidedOwnership passes to the person's team lead, to confirm under "To sort out"; the stage doesn't change.
12

A measurement gap is closed, and a year passes

Yuki Tanaka, Observability · Mean time to detect, a Gap metric · then the library's first anniversary

  1. Time to detect has been a Gap (MS) since the register load: nobody records an incident's first bad data point. Its goal went in held to no number, and Draft never blocked it.
  2. In March the incident tracker gains a "first bad data point" field. Yuki sets the metric to Measured, adds the report link and window, and it becomes Defined. Its goal starts counting from its first reading.
  3. A year after set-up, 9 of the 13 metrics were verified in the same fortnight. From 30 days before, "To verify" lists them as "due 3 Nov", so Lucía and Amara spread them over the month instead of all lapsing on one day.
  4. Two still lapse because nobody got to them. They drop to Defined with the reason "12 months since verified", and their goals don't change. Only Draft warns.
Cost

About 3 hours of verifying a year for the division, spread over a month.

Found

Gap and Draft are different questions (is it measured; is it defined) and both must stay visible without blocking. The yearly lapse needs the 30-day warning or it lands as a wave.
