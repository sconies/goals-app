# Faro usability study

**Platform Engineering demo · 17 November 2026**

## Executive summary

Faro has the information needed to manage by exception. It is hardest to use when the same screen asks the reader to scan a portfolio, diagnose a goal and decide what to do. Those jobs need different amounts of information. Giving each section more padding would lengthen the mobile pages while leaving the same decisions buried.

Start with the weekly loop. Put **Check in one by one** and **Enter numbers in bulk** together on Check-in. Make the live review’s decision actions available near the top on mobile. Use the same compact goal row on Home, Watching, Goals List and the review agenda, with details available when a person opens a goal. On goal detail, keep current value and gap, the owner’s reason, and the next action within a small number of screenfuls.

The current design already has pieces worth reusing. Home can surface a request’s recipient and due date. Watching separates fresh attention from routine status. Desktop live review keeps the chart and decision actions visible together. Check-in distinguishes a remeasurement from missing data. Import previews a proposed change before writing it. These patterns are stronger starting points than a new set of cards.

### Highest-priority findings

| ID | Severity | What I observed | What to change |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Major | In live review, **Add the next step** was 1,525 CSS px down the page at 454 px width. Desktop pins the decision area at the bottom. On mobile, the top arrow advances without reviewing and the decision controls are in the page flow. | Keep progress and navigation lightweight. Add a persistent, labelled **Decide** action or a compact action rail that opens a sheet with the next step, risk and skip choices. |
| F02 | Major | Rhea’s seven-goal Check-in queue puts **Or type all your numbers in one table** after every item. The link began 988 CSS px from the top at 454 px. The visible primary route starts the longest off-track goal first. | Place two routes together above the queue. Use **Check in one by one** and **Enter numbers in bulk**, with the count and any off-track follow-up requirements visible before choosing. |
| F03 | Major | At 340 px, **Lead time for changes** had about 97 px for its title because Find, Demo and the avatar share the goal header. The title wraps onto three lines. At 454 px, the owner’s check-in action begins at y=367 and **Why** starts at y=1,403 after **Next steps and asks**. | Put utility actions in the shell or overflow. Give the title a full-width line. Put the owner action beside the current status summary and show a short reason and the current decision or recovery step together. |
| F04 | Major | At 340 px, the three cadence buttons in review setup extend to x=371. The page scroll width is 371 px against a 337 px client width. **Every 4 weeks** extends beyond the right edge. | Let the options wrap or stack as radio rows at small widths. Put the validation message next to the affected field and move focus to it. |
| F05 | Major | Board repeats the status in both the column header and each card, then adds cause, owner date, warning, and more warnings. The 1,048 px desktop board scrolls across a 1,448 px lane strip. At 454 px the stacked **At risk** section begins at y=4,324, after 11 off-track cards. | Add a status picker with counts on mobile, keep active column headings visible on desktop and default to compact cards showing goal, owner, value/gap and one reason or next action. |
| F06 | Major | At 454 px, the Data grid is about 410 px wide inside a 2,415 px scroll surface. The row grid begins around y=512, under search, tabs and multiple tool controls. The desktop grid also opens on Goal, Key, Level, Team and Owner, with current value and gap off to the right. | Give Data a row-focused mobile read/edit view with **Open full grid** as an explicit option. Keep the row identity visible during desktop horizontal scrolling and offer a small default column set per sheet. |
| F07 | Major | Watching repeats the attention count in the button, summary and heading. A watched goal can show green **On track** inside **Needs attention**. The example **Pull requests with AI-assisted review** has a projected miss, so the two signals are legitimate but require interpretation. | Keep tracking status and the new signal clearly named, such as **On track** and **Forecast miss**. State the reason for the attention flag on the row. Show the count in the section heading and filter when each serves a distinct purpose. |
| F08 | Major | The weekly review shows **0/56 reviewed**, **19 to discuss**, **37 with nothing flagged** and **0 of 53 checked in**, without explaining the population difference beside the numbers. The discussion queue starts around y=725 on desktop and y=1,218 at 340 px. | Rename denominators in place, for example **56 in scope**, **53 due to check in**, **19 flagged for discussion**. Put the discussion queue before the eight-week check-in history or make that history an expandable section. |

Severity reflects the effect on the observed task. Major means a recurring task is materially slower or harder to complete. Moderate means the user can proceed with extra scanning or relearning. Minor means local polish or consistency. The review cadence overflow is localized to the tested narrow screen, so I did not classify it as a product-wide blocker.

## Study method and boundaries

I used the live demo as Grace (admin), Amara (leader), Rhea (goal owner) and Oskar (team member). I navigated every primary destination and sampled repeated records across goal types, metrics, people and teams. I opened all six review agendas and all seven Workspace subpages. I compared standard desktop at **1,363 × 936 CSS px**, compact desktop at **1,090 × 749**, and the responsive phone layouts at **454 × 312** and **340 × 234**. Browser zoom produced the narrow CSS viewports, so the heights were shorter than a real phone’s. Measurements describe document position and overflow. They are not estimates of how many swipes a person would need on a handset.

I used visible screenshots, the page’s accessibility labels and element geometry. This is an expert walkthrough with one evaluator. It does not provide completion-time statistics or represent a study with employees. I worked with the running demo state, which already contained a goal draft and feedback examples. I exercised draft recovery, input validation, navigation and import preview. Saved check-ins, goal publication, review decisions, an applied import and exported file contents were outside the observed end states. Cycle rollover was not available in the demo week I reviewed. I sampled the reusable Help article template and record detail templates rather than opening every record and help article.

## Journey results

| Person and task | Walkthrough result | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhea · weekly check-in** | Home showed an overdue question and seven check-ins in navigation. Opened the queue and the off-track lead-time goal. Empty Continue returned an inline error. Entered 36 hours, advanced to the prior-reason step, reloaded and confirmed the draft resumed at step 2. Opened the changed-reason fields and discarded the test draft. In bulk, entering 36 hours revealed the reason fields in the row. | Preserve the distinction between measured unchanged, missing data and off-plan follow-up. Make bulk entry visible early. Replace the empty quotation marks in validation with a direct field message. Clarify whether **Continue** is leading to another decision or a post. |
| **Amara · triage and run the weekly review** | Home surfaced a due next step and another request. Opened the 19-goal agenda, started the live queue, opened the next-step form, cancelled, advanced to goal 2 and verified the count stayed **0 of 56 reviewed**. Opened setup, submitted an empty form and saw the name error. Selecting North star changed scope to 12 goals. | The action model works on desktop. On mobile, make it as easy to record a decision as it is to move past one. Label **Skip for now** and the top arrow consistently, or use a single skip affordance. |
| **Oskar · find a goal and see what it affects** | Used global Find for **G-1** and opened the correct goal with the keyboard once the result populated. Inspected current value, chart, history and contributing goals. Compared Watching, saved stale-goal view and Map framing. | Keep keyboard Find. Give searches a visible intermediate or empty state. Make one goal’s parent/children trace the default map task, with overview as a separate mode. |
| **Grace · set up a goal and review it** | Opened a preserved draft, used the summary’s Change links to inspect goal type, owner/team/level/dependency and metric/plan, then returned to step 4. Opened the existing goal’s edit screen. No proposal or edit was saved. | Reuse the wizard’s focused sections across creation and editing. Put plan changes, their effect and any required reason together. Make **Close and keep draft**, **Cancel**, **Propose** and **Publish** consistent with their actual outcomes. |
| **Grace · audit and import data** | Opened all five Data sheets. Opened current-table and full-workspace export choices. Pasted a synthetic one-row goal rename, verified the preview reported one changed goal and zero errors, opened the change detail and closed without saving. | **Grace · check people, metrics and administration** | Filtered People for Rhea, opened her profile and a CI/CD team page. Opened a defined metric, its edit form and Add a metric. Opened all Workspace categories, Settings, Help, Feedback and Research. | Reuse one directory filter pattern and one detail page shell. Put what an admin can do next next to each state, such as which part of a metric definition is missing. |

## Page-by-page findings

| Surface | Evidence and effect | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing and Demo menu** | The demo entry sets expectations before entry. The Demo menu combines personas, six guided tours, Research, browser-local reset and presentation options in one long panel. Mobile already has a second entry in More. | Keep the demo shortcut. Put personas first and group walkthroughs, Research and reset under distinct disclosure sections. |
| **Home** | The role-specific request is clear. On desktop, the week strip sits above it. The admin strip repeats the same current count in two adjacent tiles. Owned, team and watched goals use different row layouts, and the leader’s 13-team watch summary introduces a large matrix. | Use a single first-level action region, then **Your goals**, **Your team**, and **Watching** in the same visual order wherever available. Show counts and uppermost exceptions, with a single link to each full queue. Keep check-in completion and completion before review distinct but label their difference. |
| **Check-in queue** | Status, title, last value and button line up on desktop. Its list is narrow and readable. The bulk option is at the bottom and the queue order places At risk between two Off track items in this demo. | Lift the bulk route to the header. State whether the queue is ordered by goal priority, ownership or urgency. Use the same row geometry as other task lists. |
| **Single check-in** | The numeric step appropriately prioritizes the number and its unit. The off-track shortcut shows the previous long owner note, then an existing request, then three footer actions. The changed branch adds cause, explanation, person, date and next step. | Give the previous note a compact preview beside the existing request and let the reader expand it. Use separate grouped fields for cause and next action. Preserve draft and state restoration. |
| **Bulk check-in** | Seven aligned numeric inputs work on desktop. The 454 px mobile version uses stacked goal entries with a sticky full-width **Post check-ins** action; a row expands when off plan. | Put a count of ready and incomplete rows in the footer. Make the next required field in an expanded row explicit. Replace **Cancel** with wording that says where the user goes and whether the draft is kept. |
| **Goals List and scopes** | **All** defaults to a full tree with 126 goals. At desktop, a root combines status, value, target, gap, projection, trend explanation and descendant totals before any child. The mobile scope strip auto-scrolls to the active tab and can clip the label of the previous tab. | Show a compact tree row per root with expandable children, active exception count and one reason. Make scopes and saved views distinct. Use a visible **Views** picker on mobile or add clear scroll affordances and avoid partial labels. |
| **Watching and saved views** | Watching has an effective attention split. **What you watch** sits after the rows in List and was absent after switching to Map. The stale-goal saved link showed three matches and inherited the current Map layout rather than reseating the list. | Put **Manage watching** with the scope. Decide whether a saved view is a complete view or just a filter preset and make that explicit in its label and saved definition. |
| **Goals Table** | Desktop exposes Goal, Status, Now and Target and inserts summaries within exception rows. The lead-time row spans roughly 136 px in the saved Table. At 454 px the table exposes only Goal and Status headers. The dense table reads more like an expanded list. | Make comparison the default. Keep value and difference with the goal on mobile. Move longer context into row disclosure. Label the repeated right-aligned **Details** actions with their goal context. |
| **Goals Board** | Five lanes make the status model evident on desktop, but the On track lane is initially clipped and Done is farther right. At 454 px the lanes stack after long blocks of cards. | Use compact cards and status navigation. Allow a deliberate expanded card state that shows the reason, date and warnings. Avoid repeating the column’s status inside the same card unless the card can be viewed outside the board. |
| **Goals History** | Quarter results and Weekly status are different questions. Quarter results has strong tabular alignment at desktop, but shows result, actual and plan per quarter across five periods. Weekly status presents a second time-range control and a keyboard grid. | Preserve both views. On mobile default to one quarter or a short week window with goal labels always visible. Keep the relevant legend and time range adjacent to the grid. |
| **Goals Map** | **Fit all** renders tiny card text at 1,363 px. **Readable** improves labels but concentrates the first tree at the centre with unused space on the left and neighbouring branches clipped to the right. Sort is still visible although disabled in Map. | Open on a readable selected tree or exception path. Offer **Overview** to see all trees. Retain readable mode, collapse and troubled-tree controls. Remove inapplicable toolbar controls and put the map key behind a concise disclosure at narrow widths. |
| **Goal detail** | On a 1,363 px desktop, the 636 px main content and 380 px asks rail work, but the current metric card, section links, full reason, chart, dependency rows and history all draw attention. In the feed list the trailing 2.8-hours-behind label wraps into three lines. At phone width the utility buttons squeeze the title and the long asks section delays the owner’s reason. | Establish **Now**, **Why**, **Next action** together. Keep one current reason, decision/owner/due date and explicit status date. Put previous discussion, closed asks and full history behind meaningful expansions. Give row numeric metadata a consistent line-length budget. |
| **Chart, quarter plan and goal history** | The chart distinguishes actual, plan and projected range clearly on desktop. Separate goal comparison fields, chart labels and quarter rows repeat some values. The current chart anchor can land directly below the sticky compact goal header. | Use the same value/gap wording across the header, chart and quarter plan. Keep chart and quarter/history views adjacent. Anchor sections with sufficient scroll offset for their heading and first line. |
| **New goal and Edit goal** | New goal has four steps and a summary with direct Change links. A resumed draft adds a banner above the heading, and the final sticky action appears while publish-state choices are still below the fold. Edit goal is a long single form with a sticky Save button. | Share field sections, validation and action bar. Make publishing state visible beside the commit action, and put required change reasons beside affected fields. Use **Draft stays in this browser** only where the user needs to distinguish draft from publication. |
| **Reviews list** | Name sits under **Not started** in most rows. Session date, last held, open actions and a long full participant list are shown at equal density. | Lead with review name and next session. Show due/open counts at the trailing edge, show participant count by default and expose the roster on the agenda. |
| **Review agenda and print** | The desktop agenda uses a 696 px reading column, leaving room on the right, while follow-ups, eight weeks of readiness and the exception queue stack. The compact desktop has the same issue: queue at about y=721. Print has a distinct document layout. | Keep the print version separate. On desktop agendas, use a short readiness sidebar or disclosure so the discussion queue can sit next to carry-over actions. On mobile show the first flagged item or jump link near the start CTA. |
| **Live review** | Desktop already uses a strong two-column chart and last-discussion layout with decision actions pinned. At mobile width the critical actions are static near the bottom; progress, title, chart and prior discussion precede them. | Preserve the desktop action rail and build its narrow counterpart. Keep a shorter review header with progress and an explicit route back to the agenda. |
| **Review setup and edit** | Cadence, full tree selection and participants use three different selection treatments. All 12 root trees form a list before the people section. At 340 px the cadence row causes page overflow. | Use a single responsive option-group component. Collapse tree selection by default with a selected-count summary, or let users search the roots. Put a preview of unique goals and participant count near Save. |
| **People and Teams** | The directory is relatively calm. The desktop Team label adds a separate line and leaves the filter bottom edge misaligned with Search and Admins only. Teams still uses a People heading. Profile and team detail use small section labels, while row treatments differ from Goals and Metrics. | Give both directory tabs the same filter and results grid. Align search and filters by control edge. Use a common **People and teams** title or follow the active destination and keep the shared tabs beneath it. |
| **Metrics directory and detail** | Directory search, Unit, Used by, Filters, Sort and six stage/queue choices lead the results. **Defined 5** and **To verify 5** sit in the same strip, giving stage and work queue equal visual treatment. Metric detail’s main section labels are 12 px at mobile width, with Verification roughly 1,032 px down for Lead time. | Put state and action in different roles. Show stage in each row and one verification task filter. Give Verification a clear section heading and surface it near the metric’s status if the viewer can act on it. Keep unit, refresh and window as secondary row metadata. |
| **Add or edit metric** | The Add form shows the completeness count near y=2,253 at desktop in the observed draft. Purpose, calculation, window and source all compete in one long flow, while some forms elsewhere step through equivalent complexity. | Show the next required definition before the long list of settings. Use three grouped sections and keep Draft versus Defined feedback close to the commit controls. |
| **Activity** | When, Filters, export and eight event categories precede the first date. At 454 px the category strip is about 1,292 px inside a 449 px scroller. Counts such as 5,392 check-ins dominate the filter row. | Reuse the filter sheet and active chips from Goals. Put high-use event types first. Retain date grouping, with optional per-event details instead of a long strip of all categories. |
| **Data and export** | Data has a useful separate bulk workspace. The admin sheet feels wide even on desktop, with nested vertical and horizontal scrolling. **Saved layouts** in Data differs from **Views** in Goals. Export’s first menu offers shown rows/file formats plus an **Everything…** second step. | Use a shared toolbar but keep the grid interaction distinct. Support row detail/edit on mobile. Use stable row IDs and label the export choice **Current filtered table** or **Entire workspace** before format. |
| **Import, preview and Past imports** | The source picker is prominent. Upload/paste, two download options, requirements and history are all present on the initial page. Preview summarizes the one edited row, but the actual change is behind **Show the 1 change**. Past imports exposes status and Undo. | Keep clear stages of source, review and commit. Show one or a few diffs inline. Put the example and required columns beside the paste input. Keep Undo in the completion and history surfaces. |
| **Workspace and its seven subpages** | Desktop uses a two-level sidebar with content aligned to x=496. On mobile, About lists the other sections lower down and subsections get a back route and their own heading. Features uses smaller labels for long groups of choices. Fields and Recently removed have short empty states. | Put an explicit section picker near the page title on mobile. Keep major subsections at one typographic level and reuse the same empty-state action placement across Fields, AI access and Recently removed where applicable. |
| **Settings** | Settings uses a horizontal anchor strip, distinct from Workspace’s sidebar. The anchor strip is 486 px wide in a 433 px container at 454 px. Recording and Motion lab appear as sections but were absent from the visible anchor links. | Use the same mobile section picker as Workspace for long pages, and include every visible section or group them under a meaningful label. Keep personal appearance and navigation first. |
| **Help and What’s new** | Help offers search and relevant inline help links, then a long stack of fully described topics. At 454 px the Goals category begins around y=1,577, after two sections. What’s new is a chronological full-content stream. | Keep contextual help. Show category jump links or collapse topic groups on mobile. Use title and date with an expand affordance for older release notes. |
| **Feedback** | At desktop width, Search, Kind and Filters stack on the left of a full-width split panel, with count floating next to search. The detailed pane has a separate scroll region and status actions wrap. | Align filters in a single toolbar where room permits. Move status actions into a common row or overflow menu. Use a clear list-to-detail transition and Back affordance at narrow widths. |
| **Research** | The demo menu leads to a page with purpose, procedure and session start. At 454 px the **Start** heading sits around y=1,144. | Give repeat testers a **Start a session** jump action near the heading while keeping instructions accessible. |

## Shared patterns to standardize

| Pattern | Current variation | Proposed application |
|---|---|---|
| **Page header** | Goals puts period beside its title. Metrics and People use an explanatory line and strong CTA. Reviews uses a quiet outlined action. Mobile often repeats the destination name in the app bar and the page title, while Goal embeds global utilities inside its title row. | Define list, detail and focused-task headers. Align title and content to the same left line. Use one top-level action beside the title on desktop, and a full title before actions if space is tight. Keep app utilities outside the goal title. |
| **Section heading and spacing** | Goal and Settings use larger section headers with dividers. Metric detail and Workspace Features use 12 px labels as major headings. Watching repeats a count on adjacent lines. | Use a readable section heading plus one supporting sentence only when it adds context. Start with 24–32 px between desktop sections, 20–24 px on phone, and 8–12 px inside related groups. Align headings with the first line of content. Treat these as starting values and verify them against long names and quiet states. |
| **Scope, view and sort** | Goals and Data use segmented tabs. People uses underlined links. Metrics uses stage and action chips together. Activity has a separate strip. At 1,090 px, the Goals toolbar wraps Layout onto its own left-aligned row. | Use tabs for mutually exclusive scopes and a labelled picker for layout. Keep Filter and Sort on a stable toolbar or one common sheet. Show removable active filters directly over results. Switch toolbar mode as a group before an orphan control wraps. |
| **Goal summary row** | Check-in is status/name/action. Home can be icon/title/reason, or status/title/value/sparkline. Watching uses status/title/summary/right-aligned value. Review agenda has tinted rows with yet another alignment. | Establish **goal name**, **status plus value/gap**, and one **reason or action** as stable slots. Align values right on desktop and group them with status under the title on mobile. Use a second row only for context that changes the decision. Keep links for opening and buttons for acting. |
| **Action and draft footer** | Single check-in has Continue, then a conditional post. Bulk says Cancel and Close and keep draft. Edit goal and setup have sticky bottom buttons. Live review loses its action dock on mobile. | Share footer layout and focus/validation behaviour. Use verb-object primary labels, one back/cancel action, and a distinct close-and-keep label when draft persistence exists. Put errors near the related field and repeat a brief summary only when multiple rows need attention. |
| **Evidence and attention** | Needs attention, off-track, gap to plan, projected miss, no recent update, and a request for help can co-occur. Colour appears in multiple areas of the same row or card. | Reserve colour plus shape/text for tracking status. Express the reason for attention in plain language and keep recovery/decision status distinct. Reuse one legend where unfamiliar status language first appears. |

## Proposed order and acceptance checks

| Order | Work | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Weekly loop** | Fix cadence overflow, expose bulk check-in, simplify mobile goal header and add mobile review decision access. | At 340, 390 and 454 CSS px, setup stays inside the page width. From Check-in, both updating modes are visible before the list. In a mobile review, the facilitator can reach the next-step and risk choices without scrolling through the full chart and history. A long goal name has the full heading width. |
| **2 · Reading and triage** | Apply the shared goal row and clear exception language to Home, Watching, Goals List and review agenda. Reorder goal detail and agendas. | Verify an On track goal with a forecast miss, a stale goal with a last known value, and an off-track goal with an unresolved ask. Each row preserves the necessary context, exposes the reason for the flag and keeps numeric columns aligned on desktop. |
| **3 · Dense views** | Compact Board, make Map tree-first, add a meaningful mobile Data row view and simplify Goals History on small screens. | On a phone, switch from Off track to At risk without passing all prior cards. Trace one North star to its deepest flagged child with readable labels. Compare goal value to target on mobile Table, and update a permitted field on Data without losing the goal identity. |
| **4 · Shared page chrome** | Normalize page headers, list filters, section headings, mobile section pickers and form actions. | At 1,090 and 1,363 px, no toolbar control sits alone due to wrapping. At 340–454 px, tab labels and controls fit or provide a visible way to reach more. Keyboard focus remains visible and lands in the right place after validation or dialog dismissal. |

For a participant validation round, give owners a mixed batch of routine and off-plan updates, leaders a forecast miss that still says On track, and administrators a one-row import with a subtle change. Observe first action, wrong turns, whether people can explain the status/attention distinction, and whether they reach the expected end state without help. Retest on a real handset with the keyboard open and at a 1,920 px or wider desktop, which this walkthrough did not cover.
